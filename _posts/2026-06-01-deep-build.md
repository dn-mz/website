At the Schwarzman Scholars 10th reunion, I listened to stories of leaders who applied an inspiring level of intentionality to their leadership. Yao Ming ended the panel by saying, “But don’t listen to us. Chart your own path.”

Apart from being an incredibly witty remark, it was poignant. Not that advice is invalid, but that the most valuable experience comes from charting one's path and building deeply.

In an era of hype and optimisation, there is a rare energy in those who dig deep. A snapshot from the reunion: friends applying AI to power grids, rethinking how people travel, building extremely reliable automation processes, providing a voice to those facing global tragedies, and dear friends parenting with intentionality across cultures.

I then spent the next few weeks building deeply in Singapore. Testing electrical circuits with a multimeter. Working out the kinematics of swerve control. Battling latency in cross-border teleoperation.

This culminated at the AI Engineer Conference, where [The Robot Company](https://www.therobotcompany.ai/){: .custom-link} showcased our robots and teleoperation stacks (including teleoperating robot arms in London from Singapore!). We also shared our thesis on the path to autonomy on stage. At the conference, Minister Vivian Balakrishnan also shared a quote that perfectly complements Yao Ming’s: “You cannot govern a technology that you have only been briefed on.”

You cannot govern with only the briefing. You cannot chart a path with headline advice alone. Build deeply.
